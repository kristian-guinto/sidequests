Singapore presents one of the most unique electricity market architectures in the world. As a compact city-state with virtually no domestic fossil fuel extraction or hydro resources, Singapore has engineered one of the most reliable and gas-dependent grids globally.

In our telemetry records for July 2026, natural gas accounted for **95.9%** of total generation, with Combined Cycle Gas Turbines (CCGT) setting the marginal price across nearly every half-hour trading interval in the National Electricity Market of Singapore (NEMS).

## The Midday Solar Expansion

Despite land constraints, rooftop solar and floating solar arrays (such as the Tengeh Reservoir installation) are beginning to make visible dents in daytime dispatch.

### Key Market Observations:
1. **Tight Spot Price Bounds**: Unlike the extreme swings seen in island grids like the Philippines, Singapore's Uniform Singapore Energy Price (USEP) stays tightly clustered between **S$88/MWh** and **S$350/MWh** for over 90% of intervals.
2. **Peaker Scarcity Spikes**: During peak afternoon commercial cooling hours, if a gas turbine drops offline for maintenance, USEP can spike rapidly toward its regulatory ceiling (reaching **S$1,473.54/MWh** in our observation window).
3. **Regional Interconnection as the Future**: With limited domestic solar acreage, Singapore's long-term decarbonization pathway hinges on regional electricity imports (such as the Lao PDR-Thailand-Malaysia-Singapore Power Integration Project).

<DataCallout type="methodology" title="USEP Spot Market Modeling">
Wholesale spot prices in Singapore are decoupled from retail electricity tariffs. While retail consumers pay a fixed EMA-regulated tariff or contracted retail rate, large industrial consumers and contestable market participants settle directly at the half-hourly USEP rate.
</DataCallout>
